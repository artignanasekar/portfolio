Context

At IBM, the research team runs research sprints: focused windows of a few days to a week where we dig into a specific topic and interview employees inside and outside IBM about products, workflows, and everything in between. Each sprint produces a large body of insights, which sits alongside PRDs, spreadsheets, websites, and our own secondary research.

Over 12 weeks as a UX Design & Research Intern, I worked on three projects aimed at one question:

How do we make research actually get used?

Problem

Collecting research is the easy part. Keeping it organized is harder, and getting it into the hands of the PMs, engineers, and designers who need it is harder still.

Insights end up scattered across transcripts, files, and tools. Teams either:

  1. Don’t know the research exists
  2. Can’t find what they need
  3. Have to spend time re-explaining context before they can use it

The result is that valuable research goes underused, and decisions get made without the evidence behind them.

Process

I designed and built all three tools using IBM Bob, GitHub Copilot, and Figma, moving from problem framing to working prototypes. Each project targeted a different point where research breaks down:

Persona Pulse

Finding it

Surfacing insights when someone needs them.

Journey Juice

Applying it

Turning research into evaluations of real user journeys.

HERD

Testing against it

Checking assumptions against existing evidence.

Solution

1. Persona Pulse

An AI-powered research platform that turns scattered user research into a searchable source of truth.

Teams can ask questions in plain language and get concise, research-grounded answers with full traceability back to the original sources. For example, a PM writing a PRD can ask “Who typically handles these responsibilities?”, then select a persona like Platform Engineer or CloudOps Architect and ask “What are their typical jobs-to-be-done with this product?”

Through MCP integration with AI agents like IBM Bob, Persona Pulse works both on the web and directly inside the IDE, so users can query research data alongside their own files without having to supply extra context.

2. Journey Juice

An AI-powered tool that evaluates user journeys and turns research into prioritized recommendations.

Users upload screenshots or a Figma link with labeled frames. The tool extracts the frames in order, evaluates each step of the journey, and returns a confidence score, strengths and weaknesses, and specific recommendations for improving individual screens and workflows.

Every finding is linked to the journey step and evidence behind it, making synthesis faster, repeatable, and scalable.

3. HERD (Hypothesis Evidence Review Dashboard)

A dashboard that tests hypotheses against existing research and generates evidence-based reports. The flow has four stages:

[1]

Input

Users enter hypotheses, which are automatically categorized as behavioral, empirical, or causal.

[2]

Sources

Users attach the materials to evaluate against, such as transcripts, PRDs, spreadsheets, files, or folders.

[3]

Review

Users confirm each hypothesis along with its category and a short summary of the attached sources.

[4]

Report

Users receive an in-depth report that rates each hypothesis as strong, moderate, or weak, explains the rating, and offers pain points, areas for improvement, and next steps.

A source breakdown covers what each source contains, who to contact, its timeline, and its unique value, with per-source scores available in a drill-down view.

Users can also generate a client-facing version of the report to share findings with stakeholders.

Outcome

Persona Pulse and HERD are being shipped internally at IBM, giving teams a direct path from research to decisions within the tools and workflows they already use.
